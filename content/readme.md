# Atharva Pandey

Hi, I'm Atharva.

I'm an engineer who ended up leading — someone who still opens the editor every day but has
spent the last several years setting technical direction, owning architecture end to end, and
building the standards and teams that make software ship reliably. I've carried the titles CTO,
tech lead, founder, and fractional CTO, but the through-line has always been the same: get into
the hard part of the system, understand it deeply, and make it work at a level everyone can
build on.

## How I work

I lead IC-first. That means I stay close to the code on the parts that matter most and build
teams around clear ownership rather than process for its own sake. I care about the unglamorous
things — the boundaries between services, the failure paths, the observability you wish you had
at 3am — because that's where systems either hold or quietly fall apart. I'd rather ship
something boring and correct than something clever that needs a rewrite in six months.

I like the moments where engineering meets business reality: a system that costs less to run,
a product people actually adopt, a backend that stops being the reason a team can't move. That's
the work I find worth doing.

## What I work in

I'm most at home in **TypeScript/Node.js, Go, and Python**, building distributed backends and
event-driven systems, with **PostgreSQL, Redis, Kafka, and AWS/Kubernetes** as the working
canvas. I've been building **AI-native systems** for a while now — LLM integration, retrieval,
structured outputs, agent and tool runtimes — and I care a lot about doing it with guardrails
that hold up in production, not just in a demo.

I also write **Rust**, though I'll be honest about the line: it's mostly in personal systems
projects like my edge gateway, not employer production code. I'd rather say that plainly than
pretend otherwise.

## How I got here

I started in backend engineering and grew into ownership — from taking end-to-end responsibility
for product areas and data pipelines, to leading backend teams, to CTO roles at startups where
the job was equal parts architecture, hiring, and deciding what *not* to build. I've worked
across India, Germany, and remote teams in the US, and I've done everything from enterprise
tooling to crypto-finance backends to a distributed execution platform I helped lead.

These days I'm building AI-native developer infrastructure on my own, and writing here about
the systems I know best.

## The writing
